Based on the information in your Galvo_info folder, I can answer your questions about the Z-axis and how it interacts with the galvo system.

1. The Purpose of Adjusting the Z-Axis
Yes, adjusting the Z-axis before printing is a standard and critical step for galvo laser systems. Here is how and why it works:

Focal Length: Galvo lasers use an F-theta lens, which concentrates the laser beam to a tiny, intense point at a very specific distance (the focal length).
Setting the Focus: Because materials have different thicknesses, you must move the Z-axis (which moves the bed or the laser head) up or down so that the top surface of your material sits exactly at this focal distance. This process is called "finding the focus."
During Printing: Once you find the correct Z-height (often by overlapping two red-dot pointers or using a physical spacer), that Z-axis position is your "focused set value." You tell the software to use this specific height, and the laser will print with maximum power and sharpness.
2. Is the Z-Axis connected to the Galvo Card with drivers?
Yes, exactly.

Your galvo controller card is responsible for sending the movement signals to the Z-axis, but it cannot power the motor directly. It uses a stepper motor driver as the middleman.

I analyzed your ProSysV10_Pin_Assignments.xlsx file, and it confirms this setup. The galvo card has dedicated hardware pins meant to connect to a Z-axis driver. Specifically, on the J1 Connector, your card outputs the following signals:

Pin 15 & 16 (Z-Pulse(+) and Z-Pulse(-)): Sends the step signals to the driver, telling the motor how much to move.
Pin 17 & 18 (Z-Dir(+) and Z-Dir(-)): Sends the direction signals to the driver, telling the motor whether to move the bed up or down.
Pin 19 (Z-ORG): Connects to the Z-axis limit switch (origin/home sensor) so the machine knows where the bed is.
Pin 20 (Z-ALM): Receives alarm signals from the driver if something goes wrong.
The complete flow is: Your Software (Galvo Studio) ➔ Galvo Card (ProSysV10) ➔ Z-Axis Stepper Driver ➔ Z-Axis Motor (Moves the Bed).
